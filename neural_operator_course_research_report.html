Ten findings that shape the course

  1. The video is not the neural operator lesson. The seed episode spends four minutes on neural operators (08:42 to 12:21) and points to the earlier episode. That earlier episode, "We have foundation models for language, not for physics" (26 August 2026, 1h23m), holds the actual explanation: function spaces, resolution flexibility, FNO as "a transformer with the Fourier transform in place of attention", FourCastNet, plasma, CO2, and the PINN contrast. Its transcript is saved as research_notes/earlier_episode_transcript.txt and is summarized in section 3.
  2. Discretization invariance has a three part formal definition, and most "resolution invariant" models fail one part. The JMLR paper (Definition 4, page 9) requires: accept any input point set, query any output point, and converge to one continuum operator as the mesh refines. Transformers and graph networks pass the first two and fail the third (page 2); Table 1 on page 5 compares neural networks, DeepONets, interpolation, and neural operators on the same properties. This is the single most important idea in the course and the one the video uses loosely as "context length equals resolution".
  3. FNO's headline results are reproducible on paper but come with three documented weaknesses. Parameter hunger at small budgets (JMLR Table 8: error 0.200 at 10³ parameters versus 0.075 for GNO), poor robustness on discontinuous problems (JMLR Table 9: advection error 0.094 under 10% input noise), and Gibbs-type failure near shocks (PDEBench: high band Fourier error rises two orders of magnitude as Burgers viscosity drops). A course that omits these produces students who over-trust the method.
  4. Multi PDE pretraining is real and quantified, but "multiple areas of physics" is not yet shown. Poseidon needs about 20 fine tuning samples where FNO needs 1024 on the median of 15 tasks. Subramanian et al. need about 64 samples with pretraining versus about 8000 from scratch for Poisson at 1e-2 error. DPOT, co-authored by Anandkumar, cuts error 19 to 52 percent. All three pretraining sets are dominated by fluid family PDEs. The one 2025 paper spanning hydrodynamic, radiative, elastic and astrophysical data reaches state of the art on 6 of 12 domains. MPP's authors state multi task training "does hurt performance on individual tasks". Details in section 6.
  5. Autoregressive rollout is a separate, partly solved problem. The video prefers "full rollout" to avoid error build up. The public record shows the mechanisms: MeshGraphNets uses training noise injection, PDEBench and Brandstetter et al. use the pushforward trick (up to 58x error reduction on Burgers), and DPOT still has 33.5 percent long rollout error after pretraining. PDEBench also shows FNO error grows monotonically past the trained time window.
  6. PINN failures are optimization failures, not capacity failures. Krishnapriyan et al. show a standard PINN reaches about 100 percent error on 1D convection once the speed passes about 10, and curriculum training cuts that 44x. This is why PINO and physics informed DeepONet exist. It is also why the video's "laws of physics as dense feedback" needs the qualifier that the feedback shapes an ill conditioned loss.
  7. The "physics as self improvement signal" idea has thin but real public precedent. A 2023 workshop paper reaches 1.07x and 1.02x the error of fully supervised FNO on Burgers and Darcy using only PDE residual pseudo labels. Two 2025 to 2026 papers add reward driven inference time scaling and physics informed fine tuning of PDE foundation models. None is at the scale the company claims.
  8. The company's claims are self reported. Accelerated Understanding launched 25 August 2026. The trillion parameter, five trillion context, 22 terabyte output, and "broad beats narrow" statements have no public benchmark. One trade headline says so directly: "No Benchmark Proof Yet". No funding or investors are disclosed. No Anandkumar group paper on semiconductor physics exists in the public record.
  9. No existing course covers the gap. ETH Zurich's "AI in the Sciences and Engineering" is the closest analogue and covers PINN, FNO, CNO, GNN and transformers with public notebooks. Nothing found teaches multi physics foundation models, resolution invariance tested in practice, aliasing, rollout metrics, physics based self improvement, or an FNO versus DeepONet versus CNO versus GNN comparison on shared benchmarks. Those seven gaps define the course's contribution.
  10. The three companion documents already partition the work. The theory plan owns the mathematics. The project kit owns the first experiment. The new course should own the middle: architecture, evaluation, transfer evidence, and applications, and should hand off to the other two at defined points.

What the earlier episode adds

The 26 August episode is the primary source for "neural operators as discussed in the video". Timestamps refer to the cleaned transcript at research_notes/earlier_episode_transcript.txt.

The mental model (00:16:12 to 00:19:07)

Standard networks map fixed size vectors. Neural operators map functions to functions, so input and output can be resolved at any resolution. Zero shot super resolution is guided by adding PDE constraints as loss terms at the higher resolution, not as hard constraints.

Why not PINNs (00:11:58 to 00:15:45)

A PINN solves one PDE instance by optimization, and that landscape is often intractable for time dependent or turbulent problems. Neural operators train on many instances first and can still add physics at test time: "data driven and physics informed together".

Why Fourier (00:19:19 to 00:25:53)

The Fourier domain captures non local effects at quasi linear cost. The architecture keeps nonlinearity, residual connections and channel lifting around the Fourier layers so the model finds a latent space, not just the raw Fourier basis. She describes it as a transformer with the Fourier transform where attention would be.

The scale argument (01:09:14 to 01:11:47)

Industrial grids start near 1000 points per dimension. With time that gives hundreds of billions to a trillion context. Video models run at lower resolution and optimize for looking good, not fidelity.

FourCastNet facts (00:28:25, 00:35:36 to 00:38:30, 01:00:03 to 01:02:21)

About 50,000 training samples at 0.25 degree. Trained to predict six hours ahead with a little multi step fine tuning, yet stable for months. Flat projections destabilize long rollouts; FourCastNet 3 uses spherical harmonics. She concedes residual instability near the poles.

Plasma, CO2, geometry, inverse design (00:43:44, 01:04:08 to 01:06:59, 01:10:36 to 01:17:15)

Tokamak MHD digital twin a million times faster from a few thousand samples, with the UK Atomic Energy Authority. CO2 plume migration over decades. Cars and aircraft mapped into a shared latent geometry. Inverse lithography masks and quantum dot gates as design examples.

Correction to the study kit's framing. The catheter design example does not appear in the earlier episode transcript. It comes from the seed episode's secondary coverage and from the 2023 paper (arXiv 2304.14554). The open problem she names directly (00:41:14 to 00:42:03): enforcing physical constraints through very long rollouts without washing out fine detail or keeping unphysical detail.

The core papers, distilled for teaching

All four local PDFs were read in full. The DeepONet and PINN papers were read from arXiv. Numbers are quoted from the papers with page references.

Fourier Neural Operator (Li et al., ICLR 2021, arXiv 2010.08895)

The layer is v ↦ σ(Wv + F⁻¹(R · Fv) + b), with R a learned complex tensor on the lowest k_max modes per axis (Definition 3, page 5). The convolution theorem is the whole trick: a translation invariant kernel is diagonal in Fourier space, so the O(n²) integral becomes O(n log n). Four Fourier layers, k_max 12 in 2D, width 32, ReLU, Adam, 500 epochs (page 6). The local term W is what handles non periodic boundaries (page 9).

Teachable moments. Truncating modes does not band limit the output: truncating a Navier Stokes solution at 20 modes gives about 2% error, yet FNO with 12 modes reaches at most 1%, because the nonlinearities regenerate high frequencies (Appendix A.2, page 13). Discretization invariance is a property of the representation: R lives in Fourier space and the basis functions exist everywhere (page 6). The stated limitation is data cost: ν=1e-4 needed 10,000 simulator solves (page 9).

Neural Operator: learning maps between function spaces (Kovachki et al., JMLR 2023)

The general layer sums a local linear operator, a non local integral kernel operator, and a bias function, then applies a pointwise nonlinearity (Equation 6, page 10). Four ways to compute the O(J²) kernel cheaply: Nyström and truncation in graph form (GNO), rank r factorization (LNO, O(J)), a fast multipole hierarchy (MGNO, O(J)), and the Fourier convolution (FNO, O(J log J)) (Section 4, pages 12 to 24). Proposition 5 (page 25) shows DeepONet is a neural operator with a pointwise first kernel, which is why it is not discretization invariant. Proposition 6 (page 27) shows transformer attention is a neural operator layer with a data dependent kernel.

Theory to state exactly. Theorem 8 (page 54): discretization invariance for neural operators on Banach spaces continuously embedded in C(D̄) with continuous activations. Theorems 11 to 14 (pages 56 to 57): universal approximation for continuous operators between Lebesgue, Sobolev, and continuous function spaces (Assumptions 9 and 10, page 55), in worst case over compact sets and in Bochner norm. The authors state twice that the proofs reduce to linear approximation and do not explain the empirical benefit of nonlinear approximation or depth (pages 52, 58). The four sources of error (pages 50 to 51): approximation, discretization, finite sample, optimization. The theorems control the first two only.

DeepONet (Lu, Jin, Karniadakis, arXiv 1910.03193; Nature Machine Intelligence 2021)

A branch net reads the input function at m fixed sensors and a trunk net reads the query location; the output is their dot product plus a bias (Equation 2). The design implements the Chen and Chen 1995 operator approximation theorem. Reported rates: below about 10⁴ training samples the error falls exponentially, above that the generalization error falls as x⁻¹ (page 12). The journal version adds two authors; its body is paywalled and was not read. Lanthaler, Mishra and Karniadakis (arXiv 2102.09618) split total error into encoding, approximation, and reconstruction (Theorem 3.3), bound reconstruction error below by the tail of the output covariance spectrum (Theorems 3.6 and 3.8), and prove algebraic rather than exponential network growth for four concrete PDE operators.

Physics informed neural networks (Raissi, Perdikaris, Karniadakis, JCP 2019; arXiv 1711.10561 and 1711.10566)

One network represents one solution u(t,x); the PDE residual is computed by automatic differentiation and added to the loss at collocation points. Burgers with 100 data points and 10,000 collocation points reaches 6.7×10⁻⁴ relative L2 (Part I). Part II learns unknown coefficients; Burgers viscosity error is 0.469% noise free and 6.391% at 10% noise. The authors state PINNs are not replacements for classical solvers and that collocation cost grows exponentially with dimension. Krishnapriyan et al. (arXiv 2109.01050) show the failure at higher coefficients is an optimization problem with condition number scaling as (βN)² for convection and (νN²)² for diffusion, and that curriculum or time marching training recovers 39x to 44x.

MeshGraphNets (Pfaff et al., ICLR 2021, arXiv 2010.03409)

Encode, process, decode on a graph built from the mesh, with a second set of world space edges for contact (radius r_W). Edges carry relative displacements, which gives equivariance. The decoder predicts a derivative, integrated by forward Euler. Fifteen message passing steps, history of zero or one step, noise injection during training for stable rollouts (pages 3 to 5, 14 to 15). The model learns its own sizing field for adaptive remeshing (Section 3.2).

"The standard error measure in ML, the RMSE on test data, is not a good proxy for evaluating ML models, in particular in turbulent and non-smooth regimes" (PDEBench page 3). The hybrids and the alias question

  • PINO (arXiv 2111.03794): data loss on a coarse grid plus PDE loss on a fine grid, derivatives taken exactly in Fourier space. Kolmogorov flow: PINN 18.7% error in 4577 s, PINO 0.9% in 536 s. Darcy with 11×11 data and 61×61 constraint: 1.56% versus 9.46% data only. Burgers with no data at all: 1.50%.
  • Physics informed DeepONet (arXiv 2103.10974): trains a DeepONet family from initial and boundary data alone. Loss weight λ swept by hand over six values for Burgers; the authors pose "what are appropriate weights" as open.
  • Representation equivalent neural operators (arXiv 2305.19913): a formal alias free criterion. FNO is not alias free in practice because pointwise nonlinearities create frequencies outside the band. CNO is built alias free by design. DeepONet is alias free only with uniform sensors.
  • Operator learning survey (Kovachki, Lanthaler, Stuart, arXiv 2402.15715): two paradigms, encoder decoder nets and neural operator layers; universality settled, quantitative complexity open outside linear and holomorphic operators; no optimization theory.

Verdict for the course. Teach the narrow claim as established: pretraining on a family of related PDEs cuts downstream data needs by one to two orders of magnitude in the low data regime, across four independent groups. Teach the broad claim as open: no public model spans disparate physics with uniform gains, multi task interference is documented, and the advantage fades with distribution shift and with abundant downstream data. Weather is the one domain where multi source, multi resolution foundation models already beat the best classical system on operational data: GraphCast beats ECMWF HRES on 90% of 1380 targets, GenCast beats ENS on 97.2% of 1320 targets.

Rollout stability and physics as signal

  • Pushforward trick (Brandstetter, Worrall, Welling, ICLR 2022, arXiv 2202.03376): unroll two steps, backpropagate only the second. FNO with pushforward and temporal bundling: error 0.51 versus 29.98 unrolled, about 58x. Plain Gaussian noise "performs worse than no stabilization" in that paper, while MeshGraphNets relies on tuned noise. Both are worth one lab.
  • Self improvement (Majumdar et al., NeurIPS 2023 workshop, arXiv 2311.13885): residual only pseudo labeling on FNO reaches 1.07x and 1.02x the supervised error on Burgers and Darcy.
  • Reward driven inference time scaling for PDE foundation models (arXiv 2509.02846) and physics informed fine tuning of PDE foundation models (ICLR 2026 workshop, arXiv 2603.15431): both state that existing models "struggle with auto-regressive rollout performance, especially in out-of-distribution cases". Numbers not extracted; abstracts only.
  • Datasets for the next experiments: The Well (NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2412.00568; 16 domains, 15 TB), PDEArena (arXiv 2209.15616), PDEBench, and APEBench.

The seven confirmed gaps. No surveyed resource teaches (1) multi physics foundation models, (2) resolution invariance verified and broken in practice, (3) aliasing in spectral layers, (4) evaluation and rollout metrics for autoregressive operators, (5) an industrial vertical such as semiconductor simulation, (6) physics based self improvement loops, (7) FNO versus DeepONet versus CNO versus GNN on shared benchmarks. Caltech CS159 could not be confirmed as a current neural operator course.

Candidate course skeleton

Thirteen modules. Each names its primary sources, the lab that would go with it, and what it hands to the theory plan or the project kit. Nothing here is built. The labs are proposals.

0. Orientation: three objectives, one vocabulary

Sets the distinction the study kit already draws: surrogate, PINN, cross domain foundation model.

Sources: seed episode 00:55 to 04:52; earlier episode 00:11:58 to 00:19:07; JMLR Section 1.1. Prerequisite check: Trefethen chapters on Fourier differentiation; the diagnostic from the theory plan. Hand off: theory plan Modules 1 to 3 for function spaces and Green's functions.

1. Functions in, functions out: discretization invariance

The idea the video calls "resolution equals context length", stated exactly.

Sources: JMLR Definitions 1 to 4 and Table 1 (pages 5 to 9), Theorem 8 (page 54); ReNO (2305.19913) for the alias free criterion. Lab: take one trained FNO from neuraloperator and evaluate it at three grids. Then train a plain CNN with a fixed filter size in grid points and evaluate it at the same three grids: its physical support shrinks as the grid refines, so it fails requirement three (JMLR Section 4.1, pages 15 to 16). Gap covered: 2 and 3.

2. The Fourier layer from the convolution theorem

One layer, derived, then trained.

Sources: FNO Definitions 1 to 3, Figure 2, Appendix A.2; earlier episode 00:19:19 to 00:25:53. Lab: reproduce FNO Table 3 on Burgers at two resolutions with neuraloperator, record k_max, width, error, and the spectrum of the output. This is the project kit's step 01.

3. Four answers to one cost problem

GNO, LNO, MGNO, FNO as Nyström, low rank, multipole, and FFT.

Sources: JMLR Section 4, Tables 6 to 8; Multipole GNO; Laplace and Wavelet NO as "swap the transform". Lab: refinability curve on Darcy at 10³ to 10⁶ parameters for FNO and one kernel network method. Gap covered: 7.

4. DeepONet, PINN, and the hybrids

Encoder approximator decoder versus operator layers; constraint versus amortization.

Sources: DeepONet Equation 2 and Section 4; Lanthaler error decomposition (Theorem 3.3); PINN Parts I and II; Krishnapriyan failure modes; PINO Table 4; physics informed DeepONet. Lab: DeepXDE DeepONet on the antiderivative, then a PINN on convection at β equals 1, 10, 30, then the curriculum fix. Gap covered: 7.

5. What the theorems say and do not say

Universal approximation is existence, not efficiency.

Sources: JMLR Theorems 11 to 14 with Assumptions 9 and 10; Kovachki, Lanthaler, Mishra FNO bounds (2107.07562, poly logarithmic growth for Darcy and Navier Stokes, super exponential worst case); operator learning survey (2402.15715). Hand off: theory plan Modules 4 to 10 own the proofs. This module states results and the four sources of error.

6. Meshes, geometry, spheres, and attention

The host's question at 08:44: how does transfer work across geometries?

Sources: MeshGraphNets Sections 3 and 5; GINO; Transolver and Transolver++; CNO; Spherical FNO; JMLR Proposition 6. Lab: PhysicsNeMo's MeshGraphNet on its structural_mechanics/deforming_plate example, which mirrors the paper's dataset; read DeepMind's TensorFlow 1 release for the loss and noise schedule; ablate world edges. The community PyTorch ports were found by search only and are not vetted. Earlier episode 01:11:36 on shared latent geometry.

7. Time: one step, many steps, or the whole trajectory

The video's "full rollout" position, tested.

Sources: FNO-2D versus FNO-3D (FNO page 8); MeshGraphNets noise injection (pages 14 to 15); pushforward trick (2202.03376); PDEBench Section 4.3 and Figure 5c; DPOT long rollout numbers. Lab: train one step, unroll 50 steps, then add noise injection and pushforward; plot error versus horizon. Gap covered: 4.

8. Evaluation that can fail a model

RMSE is not a report card.

Sources: PDEBench metrics (Appendix B), failure modes (Figure 3), ground truth error (Appendix H), fair speed up caveats in both PDEBench and MeshGraphNets; JMLR Table 9 noise robustness. Lab: compute cRMSE, bRMSE and band fRMSE for the Module 2 and 7 models; split by initial condition family. This is the project kit's evaluation section.

9. Scale: memory, modes, sharding, and the trillion context arithmetic

Where the company's numbers come from and what public work reaches.

Sources: seed episode 04:52 to 07:40 and 12:21 to 15:00; MG-TFNO (150x compression); Incremental FNO; U-NO; Transolver++ million point meshes; FNO cost O(n log n). Teach the nuance: the quadratic cost argument rules out vanilla attention, not attention itself; MPP, Poseidon, DPOT, and Aurora are attention based with patching or Fourier attention. Lab: measure memory and time of one FNO forward pass versus grid size and mode count; extrapolate to 1000³. Reading: FourCastNet 3's 60 day forecast under 4 minutes on one GPU.

10. Physics foundation models and the transfer question

The course's central research module.

Sources: section 6 in full: MPP, Poseidon, DPOT, UPT, Subramanian et al., ICON, The Well, Aurora, GraphCast, GenCast. Lab: the project kit's experiment matrix A to D on Burgers and advection diffusion, with seeds and data fractions 1 to 100 percent. Gap covered: 1.

11. Physics as data engine and as reward

Simulator curricula and dense feedback, with the optimization caveat.

Sources: seed episode 15:00 to 18:30; Krishnapriyan curriculum; Incremental FNO; PINO; self improving PINO (2311.13885); reward driven inference time scaling (2509.02846); physics informed fine tuning (2603.15431). Lab: add a normalized residual penalty to the Module 10 model and sweep its weight; report out of distribution error. Gap covered: 6.

12. Applications and industry

What is public, what is claimed.

Sources: FourCastNet lineage; plasma FNO; nested FNO for CO2; catheter design; seismic inversion; UPT and GINO for automotive; company statements and press for semiconductors and geothermal; earlier episode 01:04:08 to 01:17:15. Reading task: write the evidence ledger for one application, company claim versus paper. Gap covered: 5, as far as the record allows.

Capstone

The project kit's "Operator Transfer Under Physics Shift", unchanged.

Deliverable: a transfer map with seeds, splits, invariants, and negative results. The course ends where the kit begins.

Questions only you can answer

  1. Audience. Is the course for you alone, for a public audience, or for engineers in one industry? The theory plan assumes an MIT PhD. A public course needs Module 0 expanded and Module 5 shortened.
  2. Math depth. Should this course state theorems and defer proofs to the theory plan, as drafted, or absorb the theory plan into it?
  3. Domain anchor. Semiconductors have no public neural operator literature from the group. Weather and fluids have the deepest public record. Fusion and CO2 sit between. Which one should anchor Modules 6, 10 and 12?
  4. Format. HTML lessons like the companion files, notebooks, recorded video, or all three? Labs assume PyTorch and neuraloperator; is Julia or JAX in scope?
  5. Length. Thirteen modules at one week each, or a compressed six week version that drops Modules 3, 6 and 9?
  6. Company claims. Should the course present Accelerated Understanding's statements as a case study with an explicit evidence grade, or leave the company out and keep only the papers?
  7. Next step. Build the lesson pages first, or run the project kit's Burgers baseline first so the labs have real numbers?
