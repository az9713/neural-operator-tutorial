How to use this course

Each module is one HTML page and, where a lab exists, one notebook in ../notebooks/. Every page has the same six parts in the same order: why the module exists, the core ideas in prose, the mathematics with the derivations written out, the lab with real numbers, the sources with line citations, and an exit test. Do the exit test with the page closed. If you cannot pass it, reread the section it names rather than the whole page.

The pages are written on one rule, and the rule explains why they are longer than they need to be. A stated fact without its argument is treated as a defect. So every substantive claim carries a precise statement, a definition of every term inside it, a proof in the smallest setting where the mechanism is visible, the rescue or the limit case, and a tie to a line of code, a page of a paper, or a number from a lab in this folder. Module 0's Fact 1 is the worked model of that shape, and everything else is held to it.

Three objectives run through the whole course and must never be mixed, because each is refuted by a different experiment. A surrogate reproduces a known simulator faster. A PINN uses the equation as a loss to fit one solution. A foundation model reuses one representation across several physical systems. Success at one proves nothing about the other two. Module 0 fixes the vocabulary, Module 10 tests the third with an experiment designed so that it can fail, and Module 12 grades a company's claims against all three.

The theory plan of 4 September 2026 (a private study plan, not in this repository) is absorbed into these pages. Its proofs sit in Modules 0 to 5. The mapping is decision 2 of the project notes, also not in this repository.

The labs

Three labs have run and their numbers appear on the pages, each with its script, seed and results file. The rest are specified in full on their module page and in the notebooks, and are marked "not yet run" wherever they appear. No page in this course carries a plausible-looking number in place of a measured one.

Lab 01, Burgers (ran)

../labs/01_burgers/. Run A puts the bundled 16-point space-time data through the library's own loader and Trainer. Run B implements the ICLR and JMLR paper specification, ν = 0.1 on (0, 2π), with a solver written and checked in the same file, trained at 64 points and evaluated at 64, 128, 256 and 1024. Run C is the CNN counterexample.

Lab 02, die thermal (ran)

../labs/02_thermal/. Steady heat conduction in a 10 mm silicon die with random rectangular power blocks. The finite-difference truth is checked against an analytic solution to \(1.25\times10^{-5}\). An FNO is trained at 64² and evaluated at 64² and 128², with hot-spot error reported in kelvin and millimetres.

Lab 03, scale (ran)

../labs/03_scale/. One 2D FNO forward pass from 64² to 1024² at n_modes 16 and 64, recording peak memory and time per grid, then extrapolating to 1000³. The numbers are in Module 9 and they are what makes the scale claims costable.

What the labs found

The labs were built to make the course checkable, and three of their results should be known before you start, because several later modules depend on them.

  1. Resolution invariance describes the architecture, not the error. An FNO trained at 64 points and evaluated at 1024 keeps a single Fourier coefficient of magnitude \(1.44\times10^{-2}\) at the Nyquist bin, identical at every unseen grid, which accounts for about 89 percent of the error rise from 0.0224 to 0.0349. The same defect appears in two dimensions as visible stripes and a 27-fold rise in the high-frequency error band. Modules 2, 6 and 8.
  2. A CNN is not bad at this, until the grid changes. A convolutional network with a 49-point stencil matches the FNO exactly at the training grid, 0.0224 against 0.0224, on 58 percent of the parameters, and then reaches 1.46 at 1024 points, which is worse than predicting zero. Module 1.
  3. The library counts Fourier modes differently from the paper. neuraloperator 2.0.0 keeps n_modes // 2 + 1 bins on the real-FFT axis, so n_modes=16 gives \(k_{\max}=8\) and reproducing the paper's \(k_{\max}=16\) needs n_modes=32. Anyone who types the paper's number into the library trains a model with half the spectral width. Module 2.

Two corrections made during the rewrite are logged at the end of ../research_notes/pdf-fno-jmlr.md and are flagged here, since both had propagated across several pages. The Burgers FNO row is not identical between the two versions of the paper: ICLR 2021 reports 0.0149 to 0.0139 and JMLR 2023 reports 0.0018 flat, a factor of about eight, while every other row of both tables does match. And the aliased energy fraction of Lab 01's training grid is 0.15 percent, not the 0.5 percent three pages previously said.
