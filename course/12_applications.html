Why this module exists

Decision 6 of this course keeps Accelerated Understanding as a case study with an explicit evidence grade on every claim. Decision 3 anchors the course in semiconductors, where the public neural operator literature from the Anandkumar group is empty (research_notes/lineage.md:328-329). Both decisions need the same skill: reading a speed up or accuracy claim and asking what was measured, against what, on which hardware, by whom.

The Bayesian inverse problem in the FNO paper itself is the model for how to read a speed up: 0.005 s per FNO evaluation against 2.2 s for the pseudo spectral solver on a 256² grid, both on GPU, 30,000 evaluations, 2.5 minutes against over 18 hours, and the paper counts the 12 hours of data generation and training against the FNO and still finds it faster for repeated use (research_notes/pdf-fno-jmlr.md:159-172). The two benchmark papers add the standard of honesty: MeshGraphNets, "we find it hard to provide a 'true' hardware-agnostic performance comparison"; PDEBench, "Whether or not this constitutes a fair comparison is surely debatable" (research_notes/pdf-mgn-pdebench.md:331).

Semiconductors: the anchor with no public literature

A search for Anandkumar group neural operator work on semiconductor or chip physics returned nothing. Two papers from other groups apply physics informed operators to EUV lithography mask simulation: "Physics-Informed Neural Operator for Warm-Starting Background-Decomposed and Preconditioned PSFD: Enabling Scalable 3-D EUV Mask Simulation" and "Physics-informed neural networks and neural operators for a study of EUV electromagnetic wave diffraction from a lithography mask". Their arXiv identifiers were not verified and must be before either is cited (research_notes/lineage.md:328-329). The company names chip design as its first market: physics modelling of chip performance under heat and materials constraints (company-episode.md:126-128; seed episode 20:27 to 22:58). Anandkumar names inverse lithography mask design and quantum dot gate design as inverse design examples in the earlier episode (01:16:00 to 01:17:15, :81).

What this course does about it. It generates its own semiconductor data. Lab 02 (Module 6) solves steady heat conduction in a 10 mm silicon die with random rectangular power blocks, the simplest physics behind "faster chips that don't melt", with a finite difference solver checked against an analytic solution. Every parameter is a modelling choice made in this folder and marked as such. It is a first physics, not the field: EUV mask diffraction is a Maxwell problem on nanometre features, drift–diffusion is a coupled nonlinear system, and neither has public neural operator data either.

Lab: write the evidence ledger for one application

LAB Reading task, one page. Pick one row of the applications table. Read the primary paper's experiment section, not the abstract. For every number in the row, record: what was measured, the reference it was measured against, the hardware on both sides, whether offline cost is counted, and whether the number is a field error or a scalar. Then find the company or press statement that cites the same work and grade the distance between the two. Deliverable: a table like the one above with an added column "what I would need to see to raise the grade".
